# Career CoDesk: early product and architecture snapshot

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Version | `0.1.0-early` |
| Date | 17 July 2026 |
| Status | Exploration baseline; not approved for implementation |
| Market | UK further education and post-16 careers provision |
| Purpose | Preserve the earliest coherent product definition before further validation changes it |

## 1. Product thesis

Career CoDesk is not primarily a careers-content, assessment, or chatbot product. Its proposed role is to close the operational gap between identifying learner needs and delivering timely support with finite adviser capacity.

The current positioning is:

> Career CoDesk converts learner-needs data into a capacity-feasible, adviser-approved intervention plan and records the approved decision in the college's existing system.

The proposed value is more timely and traceable support within a fixed careers-service budget, with explicit visibility of unmet demand. Employment, retention, learner satisfaction, Gatsby, and inspection outcomes remain longer-term influenced outcomes rather than direct causal promises.

## 2. One platform with three product surfaces

The project currently implies three role-specific experiences, not three independent products.

### 2.1 Learner workspace

The learner can:

- Complete a structured career-needs check-in.
- Review an approved next step and the reason for it.
- Launch an approved self-service tool or resource.
- See deadlines and progress.
- Report that an intervention did not help.
- Request human support or correction.

The learner should not see an unexplained risk score or be silently denied personal guidance because a self-service route was proposed.

### 2.2 Adviser and manager workspace

Staff can:

- Review learner statements and MIS context separately from AI inferences.
- Approve, amend, or reject proposed classifications and routes.
- Identify cohorts with similar support needs.
- Compare demand against adviser, workshop, room, timetable, and provider capacity.
- Produce an executable weekly intervention plan.
- Prepare for guidance sessions and draft actions or follow-up records.
- See waiting time, queue age, capacity consumption, and unmet demand.

The principal staff interface should be a decision queue and operational workbench, not a generic analytics dashboard.

### 2.3 Institution control centre

Authorised administrators can configure:

- Identity, SSO, roles, and field visibility.
- College-specific need taxonomy and service catalogue.
- Minimum entitlement, maximum wait, escalation, and grouping policies.
- Adviser calendars, workshop limits, rooms, and external-provider capacity.
- MIS and other connector mappings.
- Approved AI tools, deep links, completion signals, and fallbacks.
- Model policies, retention rules, safeguarding contacts, and integration health.

This interface is operationally necessary but should be kept separate from normal adviser work.

## 3. Shared domain logic

All three surfaces operate on the same learner-support case and event history. No interface should maintain its own authoritative learner profile.

One person may have multiple enrolments and several concurrent needs. Therefore `learner`, `enrolment`, `need`, and `case` are separate concepts.

### 3.1 Core records

| Record | Meaning | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Need capture | What the learner, adviser, or MIS supplied | Source-qualified and versioned |
| Need hypothesis | AI-produced tags, interpretation, confidence, and unknowns | Provisional |
| Support decision | Approved, amended, or rejected routing | Adviser-owned |
| Intervention allocation | Self-service, group, one-to-one, or external support | Proposed by system; approved by adviser |
| Delivery event | What was actually delivered, by whom, and when | Adviser/provider record |
| Outcome confirmation | Whether the need was resolved or remains open | Learner/adviser confirmation |
| Writeback transaction | What was sent to the institutional system and whether it succeeded | Connector audit record |

MIS information should be treated as a source-qualified institutional record, not an infallible fact. Corrections and conflicting sources must remain visible.

### 3.2 Initial workflow state

```text
captured
  -> interpreted
  -> awaiting adviser review
  -> approved / amended / rejected
  -> allocated
  -> scheduled or active
  -> delivered
  -> learner-confirmed
  -> closed or reopened
```

A safeguarding or serious-welfare disclosure exits the ordinary career workflow immediately and enters a restricted college-owned process. Career barriers and safety signals must not share one score or queue.

### 3.3 Cross-interaction example

1. A learner reports interest in apprenticeships and uncertainty about finance.
2. AI proposes transparent barrier tags while retaining the original response.
3. The system finds a similar cohort and proposes a funding workshop plus an approved resource.
4. Deterministic rules check deadlines, entitlement, adviser hours, rooms, and group limits.
5. An adviser approves or changes the plan.
6. The learner receives one clear next action.
7. Attendance, completion, and feedback are recorded as separate events.
8. The approved intervention record is written to the college's existing system.
9. If the learner reports that the problem remains unresolved, the case reopens for further review.

## 4. Backend and service-provider boundary

Career CoDesk requires one multi-tenant orchestration backend. It is platform infrastructure, not a fourth user-facing product and not a bespoke codebase for every college.

The initial backend needs:

- Tenant identity, SSO, and role-based access.
- A canonical learner, enrolment, need, decision, intervention, and event model.
- Human approval, override, and immutable history.
- Deterministic capacity and scheduling logic.
- A server-side AI gateway.
- A registry of approved tools and services.
- Per-MIS connector adapters.
- A durable writeback outbox with idempotency, retries, and reconciliation.
- Audit, correction, retention, security, and operational monitoring.

A modular monolith, relational database, and background job queue are sufficient for an MVP. Microservices are not an early requirement.

### 4.1 AI boundary

User interfaces must not call an AI provider directly. The server-side AI gateway owns:

- Provider credentials and model selection.
- Structured-output schemas and validation.
- Prompt, policy, and model version history.
- Data minimisation and sensitive-data controls.
- Evaluation, failure handling, latency, and cost limits.

AI may interpret, draft, explain, and propose. Deterministic code owns arithmetic, entitlement, hard constraints, and schedule feasibility. Consequential routing requires meaningful adviser review.

### 4.2 Integration boundary

Career CoDesk should not replace the statutory MIS or an existing ILP/intervention system. The provisional ownership model is:

- MIS: identity, enrolment, and institutional source records.
- Career CoDesk: AI hypotheses, proposed plans, human decisions, and orchestration audit.
- Existing ILP/intervention layer: durable approved and delivered support record.

Likely writeback targets require vendor confirmation:

- ProSolution estate: ProMonitor/ILP task, target, or support record.
- Tribal EBS estate: Ontrack ILP, pastoral, or planned-support record.
- UNIT-e estate: CEDAR intervention or workflow record.

The commercial service should separate the SaaS licence from implementation and connector work. Field mapping, identity matching, sandbox setup, permissions, historical import, security review, acceptance testing, training, and ongoing integration support are professional services.

## 5. Initial UK market finding

The market is not empty. Existing products already cover substantial parts of the proposed workflow.

### Direct and adjacent products

- **Careerpilot Pathway Planner** validates learner questionnaire -> RAG triage -> staff allocation -> booking -> adviser action -> learner timeline. It is scheduled to close permanently in December 2026, creating a migration opportunity and a warning against rebuilding a large proprietary content library. Sources: [workflow](https://www.careerpilot.org.uk/adviser-zone/introduction-to-careerpilot-and-the-pathway-planner-tool/what-is-the-pathway-planner-triage-tool), [closure](https://www.careerpilot.org.uk/adviser-zone/careerpilot-closure).
- **Compass+** occupies publicly funded careers-programme, reporting, activity, FSQ, and targeted-support territory. Current July 2026 guidance says FE colleges and ITPs cannot yet connect their MIS to the full learner-level workflow, although this is being developed. This is an opportunity but may be temporary. Source: [Compass and Compass+](https://resources.careersandenterprise.co.uk/compass).
- **Grofar** overlaps strongly in careers operations, learner passports, custom cohorts, meetings, placements, action plans, employer CRM, and MIS connection. Source: [Careers Service Software](https://grofar.com/products/careers-service-software).
- **Unifrog, Xello, and Morrisby** already provide learner exploration, profiles, tasks, careers content, applications, interactions, and reporting. They are stronger integration targets than features to reproduce. Sources: [Unifrog UK](https://www.unifrog.org/uk-schools), [Xello UK features](https://xello.world/en-gb/features-gallery/), [Morrisby Careers Platform](https://www.morrisby.com/careers-platform).
- **Purlos Outcore** and **Morrisby CourseNav** mean that a generic AI careers chatbot is not a defensible product category. Sources: [Purlos Outcore](https://www.purlos.com/outcore/), [CourseNav](https://www.coursenav.ai/).
- **ProMonitor, EBS Ontrack, UNIT-e, and CEDAR** already provide ILPs, support records, interventions, workflows, tasks, and evidence. Career CoDesk should usually write approved decisions into these layers rather than duplicate them. Sources: [EBS REST services](https://docs.ebs.tribalgroup.com/Content/REST%20Services/REST%20Services.htm), [CEDAR](https://www.parentpaygroup.com/products/engagement-for-post-16-education/).

### Proposed market whitespace

No reviewed public evidence demonstrated the complete combination of:

1. Learner-stated support need.
2. Explicit separation of source information and AI inference.
3. College-defined service policy and entitlement.
4. Real adviser, workshop, room, and provider capacity.
5. Explainable proposed allocation.
6. Human approval and override.
7. Delivery and learner confirmation.
8. Durable writeback and unmet-demand visibility.

This is the proposed defensible core. Absence from public evidence is not proof that a vendor lacks the capability behind a sales demo.

## 6. Initial UI principles

### Learner

- Use a warm, low-density experience with one prominent next step.
- Show estimated effort, deadline, reason, and a human-help option.
- Use cards and progress states rather than exposing a large tool matrix.
- Never hide a consequential recommendation inside a chat transcript.

### Adviser

- Use a high-density, sortable decision queue.
- Show the learner/MIS source, AI inference, uncertainty, alternatives, and capacity effect together.
- Expose approvals, overrides, queue age, waiting-time risks, unmet demand, and failed writebacks.
- Pair every colour with a label and explanation.

### Manager

- Prioritise demand versus capacity, waiting time, service coverage, and subgroup differences over generic engagement charts.

### External provider

- Prefer secure, narrowly scoped confirmation forms or one-use links before building a full provider portal.

## 7. MVP boundary

The proposed first implementation is:

```text
read-only MIS or CSV import
  -> AI interpretation with visible provenance
  -> cohort and capacity proposal
  -> adviser review and approval
  -> executable weekly plan
  -> one structured export or writeback object
  -> one learner feedback loop
```

Include one approved self-service deep link. Defer a broad learner chatbot, proprietary careers library, provider marketplace, multiple live MIS connectors, and general bidirectional synchronisation.

## 8. Direction-setting decisions still open

1. Which first design-partner college and MIS will determine the first connector?
2. What exact institutional object receives the first approved writeback?
3. What minimum guidance entitlement can capacity never override?
4. Is learner-facing AI part of the first product or a later integration surface?
5. Which existing learner tools must CoDesk integrate with rather than replace?
6. What are the agreed meanings of urgency, complexity, readiness, priority, and safety?
7. Which onboarding and connector responsibilities are included in the licence versus professional services?
8. What evidence would falsify the product's core operational and commercial assumptions?

This snapshot should be superseded by a new version after these decisions are validated, not edited to conceal the earlier uncertainty.
